## Slide 1
PLANNED TIME: 45 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Opening. This talk asks how to use a fixed phenotyping budget more effectively in a structured perennial breeding population. The central distinction is between predicting completely untested candidates and filling missing observations for candidates already phenotyped elsewhere.

Source: Manuscript: Lubanga et al., supplied clean submission

## Slide 2
PLANNED TIME: 90 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Motivation. Miscanthus is a perennial C4 biomass crop. Multi-year evaluation and genotype by environment interaction make selection expensive. Sparse testing measures subsets of genotypes in environments, then predicts missing combinations. Ask whether performance comes from genomic prediction, previous measurements or broad population differences.

Source: Manuscript: Lubanga et al., supplied clean submission

## Slide 3
PLANNED TIME: 75 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Study design. The panel contains 47 M. sinensis, 56 M. sacchariflorus and 22 interspecific hybrids. Not every genotype has a phenotype in every environment. DM and FW are biomass traits; MC is moisture content. Marker filtering retained 13,687 SNPs, with 11,901 contributing to the relationship matrix used for prediction; see technical backup.

Source: Manuscript: Lubanga et al., supplied clean submission

## Slide 4
PLANNED TIME: 90 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Population structure. PC1 explains 22.57% of marker variation and separates the two species with hybrids intermediate. These groups also differ in trait means. Group differences can increase across-panel prediction correlations without establishing useful within-group ranking. Population group explained 22–34% of trial-level phenotype variation.

Source: Manuscript: Figure 1a; population genetic structure; within-group analyses

## Slide 5
PLANNED TIME: 90 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Models. Stage one estimates genotype BLUEs in each environment using site-specific mixed models. Stage two fits the three models in BGLR. Population group is a fixed effect in the main prediction analyses. L is a non-marker genotype effect, G a genomic effect. M3 adds the genomic by environment covariance. M1 has no marker information and provides a useful phenotypic baseline for CV2.

Source: Manuscript: Lubanga et al., supplied clean submission

## Slide 6
PLANNED TIME: 120 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Validation. CV1 masks all records for a genotype. CV2 masks selected genotype by environment observations while retaining other observations of that genotype. Both use random five-fold cross-validation. A missing year at a tested site is easier than prediction at an untested site. The separate two-trial analysis combines years before validation to assess transfer between sites without same-site year information.

Source: Manuscript: Lubanga et al., supplied clean submission

## Slide 7
PLANNED TIME: 75 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Variance components. In the full-data variance decomposition population group was random, unlike in the main prediction models. G×E accounts for 13.2% of total variance in DM, 16.9% in FW and 14.5% in MC under M3. Residual variance is lower in M3 relative to M1 by 50%, 59% and 45%. These are model comparisons, not guaranteed gains in predictive ability. Only five environments and three groups inform the corresponding variance estimates.

Prediction across site-years. Top panel is CV1 and bottom panel CV2. M3 generally improves DM and FW, but no model consistently wins for MC. ## Slide 9
PLANNED TIME: 90 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Worked example. Keep both the total number of measurements and the number per environment fixed. Under the all-overlap design the same 25 genotypes are measured in each of five environments. Under the all-non-overlap design five different sets of 25 genotypes are measured, representing 125 unique candidates. This is an allocation of phenotyping effort rather than a demonstrated reduction in planting, maintenance or harvesting costs. Ask the audience to distinguish the number of candidates covered from the depth of measurement for each candidate. Intermediate designs trade these two quantities. Genomic relationships connect candidates even when individual genotypes are not shared.

Source: Manuscript: Table 1; illustrative allocation schematic

## Slide 10
PLANNED TIME: 90 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Sparse design. Every design assigns 25 evaluations per environment, 125 in total. OL genotypes are measured in all five environments; each NOL genotype is measured in one environment. Thus D1 represents 125 unique genotypes, while D6 represents only 25. NOL and OL percentages describe the per-environment allocation. Designs were independently replicated ten times. The 625 possible combinations are nominal; not every combination has an observed phenotype.

Source: Manuscript: Table 1

## Slide 11

Sparse prediction. Read the x axis carefully: NOL decreases from left to right. Prediction is generally best with more NOL genotypes, with M3 highest for DM and FW in each configuration. MC does not show a consistent M3 advantage. These results do not identify a universal optimum overlap fraction because unique genotype coverage changes simultaneously.

## Slide 13
PLANNED TIME: 120 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Within-group CV1. Years were combined to give one phenotype per genotype per site, avoiding repeated-year information. With M3, across-group correlations are 0.50–0.54 but adjusted pooled within-group correlations are only 0.08–0.27 across traits and trials. These ranges summarize different trait/site combinations rather than matched endpoints. Pooled within-group evaluation centers observed and predicted phenotypes by population group. M1 is omitted because ranking untested candidates without genomic information is undefined in this analysis.

Source: Manuscript: Figure 5; Table S5 values reported in results

## Slide 14
PLANNED TIME: 120 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Within-group CV2. This analysis has one phenotype per genotype per site, so information comes from the other field trial rather than a previous year at the same trial. Adjusted pooled within-group correlations remain 0.39–0.46 for DM and FW, and 0.71–0.72 for MC under M3. The marker-free M1 already reaches 0.25–0.40 for DM/FW and 0.67–0.70 for MC. M3 improves by 0.06–0.14 and 0.02–0.04 respectively, indicating that prior phenotype information contributes substantially.

Source: Manuscript: Figure 6; across-group and within-group results

## Slide 15
PLANNED TIME: 105 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Breeder decision guide. For completely new candidates, within-group CV1 is the relevant result and is low in this panel. For established material with records elsewhere, CV2 supports filling missing phenotypes, with the two-trial analysis providing a stronger test of transfer between the two represented sites. Neither scenario demonstrates prediction at an entirely new site. These correlations describe agreement with observed BLUEs; they are not measured selection response or genetic gain. A practical next step is a prospective comparison of selected candidates, maintaining a validation set with direct measurements.

Source: Interpretation of manuscript CV1, site-year CV2 and two-trial CV2 analyses

## Slide 16
PLANNED TIME: 120 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Explain the two levels of validation slowly. First, site-year validation retains five environments and assesses missing observations or entirely withheld genotypes. In perennial material, another year of the same plants can be available under CV2. Second, the trial-level analysis collapses all years into a single BLUE for each genotype at each site. Withholding that trial-level record removes the same-site repeated-year information. Group means used for phenotype adjustment are estimated from training data only. At evaluation, observed and predicted values are centred by population group to measure pooled within-group ranking. This is a pooled correlation of deviations, not an average of three separate group correlations. Matched partitions make model comparisons more informative. Correlations with phenotype BLUEs do not directly estimate correlation with true breeding values or realised genetic gain.

Source: Manuscript: cross-validation and within-group assessment methods

## Slide 17
PLANNED TIME: 105 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

This is a proposed pilot rather than an intervention tested in the manuscript. Define whether the aim is to fill missing annual records, predict candidates at another represented site, or select completely untested material. Retrospectively mask complete candidate or site-year records to match that aim. Compare a marker-free baseline with genomic and genomic-by-environment models using identical partitions. Keep directly measured validation candidates to check within-group ranking and agreement among selected candidates. Test a modest change in annual measurement coverage in established trials while retaining the observations needed for validation. Measure actual labour and laboratory savings separately from plot maintenance and harvesting costs. Decisions about shared checks and replication should depend on the programme and require a design study; this manuscript does not establish a universal overlap optimum.

Source: Proposed breeding-programme application, informed by manuscript findings

## Slide 18
PLANNED TIME: 90 seconds. Audience: plant breeders.

Implications and limitations. The evidence most directly supports reducing annual measurement effort in established trials, while increasing unique genotype coverage. It does not establish that the same cost savings apply to plot establishment, annual harvest management or new sites. There was no direct economic analysis. Training panels should be larger within groups; hybrid sample size is particularly small. Independent populations and environments are required for broader validation.

Source: Manuscript: Lubanga et al., supplied clean submission

## Slide 22
BACKUP: outside the 30-minute main seminar.

Backup. In the two-trial analysis group-adjusted phenotypes subtract trial by population-group means estimated only from training data; training means are added back to predictions. Evaluation then centers both predictions and observations by group to calculate pooled within-group correlations. This evaluation centering is distinct from training adjustment. Identical partitions are used for adjusted and unadjusted analyses. BGLR two-trial runs use 8,000 iterations, 2,000 burn-in and thinning five. Five-fold validation is repeated ten times.

Source: Manuscript: Section 2.9.1; data availability
